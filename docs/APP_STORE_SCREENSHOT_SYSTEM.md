# CoachFit App Store screenshot system

> Historical creative brief. The submission-ready gallery is now in [release/screenshots](release/screenshots/README.md): six captures from the CoachFit Release build, with real seeded workout history and only a separate editorial header. Do not submit fabricated screen compositions from this earlier brief.

This replaces the earlier “screen + caption” approach. The gallery must sell the **experience of getting a workout that fits**, then prove that CoachFit carries the athlete through the workout and makes progress visible.

## What leading fitness listings teach us

Fitbod leads with adaptive programming and recovery-aware recommendations; Strong makes the in-workout logging loop unmistakable; Hevy leads with a simple action-and-outcome promise; Nike Training Club sells breadth by making distinct training modes immediately legible. CoachFit should combine those expectations into a tighter narrative:

1. It knows what the athlete has and needs.
2. It turns that into a workout for today.
3. It is calm and useful while the athlete is training.
4. The accumulated history makes the next decision better.

The competitive opening is important: most comparable apps use a subscription or in-app purchases. CoachFit’s one-time $3.99 price is persuasive, but it is **the final reassurance—not the first thing viewers see**.

## Creative rules

- Use a designed 6.9-inch canvas, not a raw screen recording. The app stays authentic, but each composition gets a photographic or tonal background, one phone crop, and one strong thought.
- Use no more than five words in a headline. Supporting copy is optional and must earn its space.
- Put the phone or cropped interface low enough to preserve generous headline space. Never shrink an entire long screen until the controls become unreadable.
- Keep the app in dark mode for screens 1–7. Dark charcoal, soft sage, warm ivory, and the existing exercise photography will read as more premium than a generic neon-gym palette.
- Vary the compositions: close interface crop, centered phone, and an intentional two-panel layout. Do not repeat the same framed phone on every card.
- Seed the exact demo history below before capture. The gallery must never show zeros, empty calendars, or a “Resume workout” state.

## The 9-frame gallery

### 1. A workout that fits today

**Job:** Explain CoachFit in one glance.

**Headline:** `Built for today.`

**Supporting line:** `Your goal, your equipment, your time.`

**Capture:** The workout builder after choosing Strength, 30 min, Dumbbells + Bench, and a balanced effort. Frame the selections and the “Build workout” action—not the whole scrolling Today screen.

**Composition:** Sage-to-charcoal background. Large type at top left. The builder is shown in a tight crop across the lower two-thirds, with selected chips and the primary action visible. Add no device bezel.

### 2. Train your way

**Job:** Prove CoachFit is more than a strength logger.

**Headline:** `One coach. Every mode.`

**Supporting line:** `Strength · Cardio · Mobility · Yoga · Stretch`

**Capture:** The workout-type choices expanded, with Strength, Cardio, Mobility, Yoga, and Stretch each visible. If the product cannot show all five in one readable native state, create a two-panel composition from two truthful crops—never a fabricated interface.

**Composition:** Warm ivory background with five small mode labels surrounding a central dark app crop. This is the only intentionally “catalog-like” frame; it makes breadth scan in under a second.

### 3. Ready for the body you bring

**Job:** Make personalization concrete, not AI-flavored hand-waving.

**Headline:** `Your recovery sets the pace.`

**Supporting line:** `Sleep, energy, soreness, and training intent.`

**Capture:** Daily check-in with a realistic selection: Okay sleep, Great energy, Some soreness, and Balanced. Include the recovery summary/map only if it is visually clean.

**Composition:** Full-bleed crop of the check-in over CoachFit’s exercise photography. Place headline in the quiet upper-left area. The selected responses should be the visual proof.

### 4. See the plan before the work

**Job:** Reduce uncertainty before starting.

**Headline:** `A clear plan. Before you start.`

**Supporting line:** `Warm-up, main work, conditioning, cooldown.`

**Capture:** A generated 42-minute dumbbell workout with expanded blocks. Use recognizable movements: Dumbbell bench press, bent-over row, Romanian deadlift, and a short cardio finish.

**Composition:** Deep charcoal. Center one tall app crop with the plan’s four blocks stepping downward. Use the sage accent only to connect the block labels; this should feel like a calm training plan, not a spreadsheet.

### 5. Move through the workout with rhythm

**Job:** Sell the live loop.

**Headline:** `Know your next set.`

**Supporting line:** `Log. Rest. Go again.`

**Capture:** Dumbbell bench press exercise detail after set 2 of 3: 20 kg × 8, RPE 8, a visible rest timer, next set prepared, and “How-to” / history available. Avoid an empty first set.

**Composition:** Tight crop—the timer and completed-set feedback should be big. This is the most utilitarian screen, so use the fewest decorative elements.

### 6. Adapt without starting over

**Job:** Demonstrate intelligence in action.

**Headline:** `Your plan can flex.`

**Supporting line:** `Swap what doesn’t fit today.`

**Capture:** Exercise swap sheet for Dumbbell bench press, showing compatible replacements for the equipped athlete (for example, Dumbbell floor press or Dumbbell overhead press). Make the selected current exercise and the available equipment legible.

**Composition:** A layered-sheet composition: dimmed workout behind, replacement options forward. The visual contrast is the story—CoachFit holds the plan while the athlete changes a detail.

### 7. See progress, not just numbers

**Job:** Show that logging creates a valuable result.

**Headline:** `Progress has a history.`

**Supporting line:** `Your strongest work, week by week.`

**Capture:** Strength tab with seeded Dumbbell bench press progression, recent PR, and weekly volume. Seed six increasing strength sessions across four weeks: 16 → 21 kg dumbbells and 18 → 23 kg rows.

**Composition:** Large chart crop on a dark canvas; add a restrained “+31% estimated strength” callout only if it is calculated from the seeded data and reflected in the interface. Otherwise use no invented percentage.

### 8. Every session leaves a trail

**Job:** Make history feel motivating and useful.

**Headline:** `The work adds up.`

**Supporting line:** `Calendar, volume, PRs, and consistency.`

**Capture:** Progress calendar showing nine completed workouts over four weeks, with a selected session’s sets beneath it. Include one cardio day so the history proves the app supports more than lifting.

**Composition:** Warm ivory field with the calendar crop featured at the top and the selected-workout card peeking from below. It should feel like a personal training journal, not a data dashboard.

### 9. Ownership, not interruption

**Job:** Close the sale after the product has already earned interest.

**Headline:** `Pay once. Keep training.`

**Supporting line:** `$3.99 · No ads · No subscriptions · No in-app purchases`

**Capture:** A clean, settled Progress or Settings screen—not a fake pricing screen.

**Composition:** Nearly black canvas, the CoachFit mark small at top, an elegant device crop, and the purchase promise below. Use this exact U.S. copy only for the U.S. storefront; replace the dollar amount in other territories with `One purchase. No ads. No subscriptions. No in-app purchases.`

## Demo-data recipe

Use an illustrative athlete named only in non-user-visible test data:

- Intermediate experience; strength/general goals; dumbbells, bench, bodyweight, and a cardio option.
- Nine completed sessions over four weeks: six strength sessions and three cardio sessions.
- Strength-session progression: Dumbbell bench press from 16 kg to 21 kg; dumbbell row from 18 kg to 23 kg; 3 working sets each, mostly 8–10 reps at RPE 7–8.
- Cardio: three 25-minute easy jogs with a modest, believable distance progression.
- One current unstarted 42-minute upper-body plan for the builder and plan screens. Do **not** leave an active “Resume workout” record in the screenshot state.

## Capture order

1. Builder selections (frame 1), then modes (frame 2), then daily check-in (frame 3).
2. Generate and review the plan (frame 4).
3. Start, log two sets, and let the rest timer run (frame 5).
4. Open the replacement sheet (frame 6).
5. Capture the seeded Strength and Calendar history states (frames 7–8).
6. Use a quiet completed/Settings state for the ownership closer (frame 9).

## What we intentionally avoid

- Raw empty states, zero metrics, debug notices, system alerts, or the simulator chrome.
- Generic gym stock photos that could belong to any app.
- A “feature inventory” screenshot that makes every option tiny.
- Claims of AI, medical outcomes, or results the app does not substantiate.
- Leading with $3.99 before the gallery has established why CoachFit is worth owning.
